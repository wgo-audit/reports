# Scalability

## Audit Question, Depth, And Evidence Boundary

This detailed review asks whether Plausible supports the library's realistic growth envelope across event ingestion, session calculation, data growth, interactive queries, monthly reporting, exports, deletion, horizontal scaling, hosted quotas, and degradation behavior. The cutoff is 2026-08-20 at onboarding start, America/Toronto. Evidence is the approved `primary-code` snapshot at commit `9cc669b97ece3ecd37fcb3950791cb3873d7944d`, Architecture and Product Value evidence, bounded Business Continuity/Cloud Security/Expense evidence, and the working assumptions of 18 properties, 2 million visits/year, 14 million pageviews/year, 25 staff, and seasonal peaks.

The assumptions are not production metrics. No deployment, live traffic, resource configuration, queue/datastore state, hosted runtime, or replacement candidate was accessed; no load or failure test was run. Source defaults and the repository's result-free k6 fixture are not capacity evidence.

## Coverage And Material Gaps

The review traced ingress acknowledgement through process-local session state and buffers, ClickHouse ingest/read/delete roles, PostgreSQL/Oban jobs, query timeouts/concurrency, dashboard and raw exports, monthly reports, partition growth/deletion, telemetry, session transfer, API/hosted quotas, and option-specific degradation. The full trace and validation worksheet are in the [capacity/degradation view](../../controls/scalability/capacity-envelope-and-degradation-view.md).

No option has a bounded operating envelope. [OI-019](../../controls/open-items.md#oi-019) routes the missing aggregate workload profile, deployed Run capacity/degradation proof, horizontal-session reconciliation, and hosted capacity evidence. [OI-002](../../controls/open-items.md#oi-002) remains unanswered, [OI-007](../../controls/open-items.md#oi-007) remains unanswered, [OI-008](../../controls/open-items.md#oi-008) remains governance, and [OI-017](../../controls/open-items.md#oi-017) remains cost/quota proof.

## Mandate-Relevant Strengths

- Source exposes configurable ingest pools and buffer thresholds, separates read/ingest/async-insert/deletion ClickHouse roles, and places background work in named queues. That gives Run concrete tuning and isolation points to validate rather than an opaque single process ([E-003](../../evidence/evidence-ledger.md#e-003), [E-047](../../evidence/evidence-ledger.md#e-047), [E-048](../../evidence/evidence-ledger.md#e-048)).
- The application has query execution ceilings, bounded dashboard-export result sizes, partition-aware deletion, queue/cache/buffer telemetry surfaces, and a dedicated full-export connection. These are degradation controls and signals, though not evidence that they are sufficient or enabled ([E-035](../../evidence/evidence-ledger.md#e-035), [E-048](../../evidence/evidence-ledger.md#e-048), [E-050](../../evidence/evidence-ledger.md#e-050)).
- The current scale question can be answered without production traffic inspection: approved aggregate monthly/daily/hourly counts plus synthetic event/report fixtures can define a representative envelope for later non-production validation ([OI-019](../../controls/open-items.md#oi-019)).

### Decision Insights

1. **Establish demand before buying capacity or adding replicas.** The annual average is arithmetically small, but it says nothing about short registration bursts, custom-event multiplication, or concurrent reporting. A wrong assumption can either overbuild Run or normalize loss/latency. Close the aggregate-profile portion of [OI-019](../../controls/open-items.md#oi-019) first.
2. **Treat horizontal scaling as a data-correctness change.** Process-local session state means a replica decision can change visit/session metrics unless routing or an equivalent state boundary is proven. Inventory the exact topology through [OI-001](../../controls/open-items.md#oi-001), then reconcile cross-process sessions in the non-production exercise before scaling application replicas.
3. **Protect collection and interactive reporting from maintenance workloads.** Monthly reports, dashboard ZIP, full export, and deletion have different timeout/concurrency behavior. Define which must win during peaks, schedule heavy work outside the critical window, and validate queue/query/storage isolation through [OI-019](../../controls/open-items.md#oi-019).
4. **Procure Subscribe against the same workload fixture, not a traffic tier alone.** Hosted quota, site/member entitlement, API/export limits, provider degradation, escalation, and service objectives must be answered together. [OI-017](../../controls/open-items.md#oi-017) supplies billable volume/quote; [OI-019](../../controls/open-items.md#oi-019) supplies capacity/service evidence.
5. **Do not credit Replace with scale.** Carry the measured peak, event mix, retention, query/report, export/deletion, recovery, and ownership envelope into a later funded shortlist; no current replacement evidence supports a comparison.

## Bounded Conclusion And Downstream Guidance

The reviewed source contains credible mechanisms to tune and observe a growing service, but no approved evidence establishes a safe throughput, data volume, response time, replica count, or degradation threshold. Run remains potentially plausible at the assumed annual scale but is not dependable-by-evidence until peak/custom-event demand, exact resources, workload isolation, process/replica session correctness, retention/growth, telemetry, and failure thresholds are verified. Subscribe transfers infrastructure care but lacks a service-capacity/entitlement/SLA proof and retains quota-related reporting exposure. Replace is unevidenced.

Maintenance Cost should price recurring measurement, queue/storage/query review, peak-readiness, and the proof/control work in OI-019 without inventing hardware or staff rates. Contributor and Vendor Value may evaluate whether hosted support/control transfer covers these duties, but must not treat public terms, a source default, a k6 fixture, annual averages, or an unknown capacity limit as proof of adequacy or bottleneck.
