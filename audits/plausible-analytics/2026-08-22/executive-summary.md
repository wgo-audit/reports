# Executive Summary

## Mandate, Boundary, And Bottom Line

The question is whether Plausible Analytics looks like the kind of engineering organization worth joining as CTO. On the public evidence, the answer is:

> **Lean positively toward accepting the role if the people, mandate, expectations, authority, and compensation are attractive. Nothing in the public record suggests a sloppy team, a poor architecture, or a product in technical distress.**

The visible evidence points to a serious, technically capable team: coherent product semantics, legible architecture, broad automated checks, substantive code review, careful supply-chain hygiene, and examples of user and security concerns being addressed. The audit found a small number of real corrections and design questions, but not a pattern of neglect or organizational dysfunction.

Private operational facts such as restore-test results, Cloud IAM configuration, bills, staffing plans, and on-call performance are normally unavailable to an external candidate. Their absence from public evidence is **neutral**. They belong to ordinary onboarding discovery if the role proceeds; they are not negative findings, proof of weak controls, or conditions that must be satisfied before accepting a job offer.

The employment decision should therefore turn primarily on normal executive-role questions: trust and working chemistry with the founders and team, clarity of the mandate, realistic expectations, decision authority, resources, and compensation. The public technical record is a reason for optimism, not a reason to presume hidden failure.

The audit used only public evidence at the approved cutoff. It inspected relevant public GitHub issues and pull requests rather than relying on activity counts. It did not access production, customer, financial, contractual, private operational, or staff evidence and therefore makes no conclusion about live control effectiveness, compliance, financial health, individual performance, staffing adequacy, or morale. This boundary limits certainty; it does not reduce the positive weight of affirmative evidence that is visible.

## Current Product And Control Position

### What is credibly strong

- **Product coherence.** The tracker, Events API, dashboard, Stats API, goals, revenue, funnels, journeys, collaboration, SSO, imports, exports, plans, and Cloud/CE boundaries are substantially represented in source and generally align with public documentation. Constraints such as import limitations, output warnings, quotas, and SSO owner recovery are visible rather than hidden. See the [capability matrix](controls/product/capability-contract-matrix.md).
- **Understandable architecture.** A single Phoenix/OTP release supervises the application, PostgreSQL owns relational/control/job state, ClickHouse owns analytics data, Oban handles asynchronous work, and tracker variants are generated from one source. These boundaries are legible even though their live topology and approved rationale remain private or unknown. See the [ADR register](controls/architecture/adr-register.md).
- **Observable engineering discipline.** The repository declares broad EE/CE test partitions, database-backed tests, browser E2E, Jest, type/static checks, migration segregation, tracker multi-browser checks, and pinned merge-group jobs that were green at the cutoff. Specific PRs show substantive review changing implementation. This is credible source-change discipline, not proof of production readiness. See the [change-safety matrix](controls/quality/change-safety-matrix.md).
- **Security fundamentals.** Reviewed authentication paths use random/digested tokens, revocable sessions, scoped tenant checks, role enforcement, and shared-link constraints. All 73 inspected GitHub Action references were full-SHA qualified; Docker bases were digest-pinned; lockfiles and Dependabot configuration exist. A critical historical Storybook RCE was removed, released, and publicly advised. See [Application Attack Paths](controls/application-security/attack-path-and-control-view.md) and [Supply Chain](controls/security/supply-chain-and-tooling.md).
- **Public correction channels.** Issues, PRs, releases, advisories, status incidents, and review history show that defects and security issues can be surfaced and corrected publicly. The evidence supports responsiveness in selected cases, not a universal cadence or closure SLA.
- **Technical breadth with visible safeguards.** Sampling, query timeouts, bounded job concurrency, persistence drop reasons, telemetry, health checks, retries, and migration utilities show deliberate attention to load and failure. They do not establish the live capacity or recovery envelope. See the [capacity view](controls/scalability/capacity-and-degradation-view.md).

### What belongs to normal post-join discovery

The following information is private by nature. These are useful first-month topics for any incoming CTO, not adverse findings about Plausible and not evidence that the associated practices are absent:

- The full commit-to-live Cloud gate, required checks, deployed digest, migration stop conditions, and exercised rollback.
- Backup scope, isolation, retention, RPO/RTO, and a completed reconciled restore, including closure of the public 2025 lost-data restoration incident.
- Effective Cloud IAM, network, secret, encryption, egress, region/residency, runtime isolation, audit logging, and claim ownership.
- End-to-end alert delivery, staffed on-call, incident command, queue recovery, customer reconciliation, and postmortem closure.
- Customer-effective plans, grandfathering, Enterprise terms, scheduled raw-export fulfillment, native-export operation, and tenant/SSO negative paths.
- Actual spend, contracts, vendor commitments, staffing cost, utilization, quotas, renewals, unit economics, and capacity headroom.
- Current accountable owners, primary/backup access, break-glass routes, and successor ability for critical services and accounts.
- Customer-effective legal/privacy scope, control assurance, exact claim versions, subprocessor records, and license/notice mapping.

The public contribution sample shows that six GitHub identities account for 83.9% of 57 supported feature-value points and annual samples need two or three identities to reach roughly 80%. This is only a **succession-verification signal**. It is not evidence about people performance, staffing, current ownership, employment, or indispensability. See [Contribution Value](controls/contributors/contribution-value.md).

## Material Risks, Unknowns, And Decisions

### Decision-Useful Conclusions

1. **The product is not a technical rescue case on the visible evidence.** Its architecture and product semantics are coherent enough to evolve, and the repository shows substantial automated checks and reviewed change. The CTO opportunity is principally to establish operational truth, connect source controls to live outcomes, and tighten a handful of high-consequence boundaries.
2. **The first inherited stop-the-line issue is CE import loss.** At the pinned commit, a cleanup-worker failure can be misclassified and trigger deletion of completed imported analytics. Issue #6515 reports realized loss; PR #6547 was still unmerged and unreviewed at the cutoff. This is **Critical / small immediate code correction**, with broader release, reconciliation, recovery, and notification work in [OI-006](controls/open-items.md#oi-006).
3. **Privacy-led differentiation increases the cost of imprecise telemetry and wording.** Source can attach raw IP and User-Agent to Sentry error context, while public copy categorically says those fields are not stored; salt wording says 24 hours while source retains current and previous salts and deletes rows older than 48 hours. These are evidence-backed claim/control conflicts, not a finding of actual disclosure or noncompliance. See [OI-015](controls/open-items.md#oi-015), [OI-011](controls/open-items.md#oi-011), and the [compliance matrix](controls/compliance-assurance/requirement-to-evidence-matrix.md).
4. **Release and operational practices are appropriate onboarding topics, not public red flags.** The public repository stops at image publication and cannot show private promotion, rollback, recovery, Cloud controls, or on-call operation. The absence is expected at this evidence boundary. The source-visible release, health, telemetry, retry, and incident primitives are affirmative signs of operational thoughtfulness. See [OI-003](controls/open-items.md#oi-003) and [OI-021](controls/open-items.md#oi-021)–[OI-024](controls/open-items.md#oi-024).
5. **The concrete negative evidence is bounded.** The CE import-cleanup defect is the most important direct correction. Sentry request context, the tracker PR credential boundary, Browserless navigation enforcement, Stats API validation, the session-buffer metric, and Events API response documentation form a meaningful but ordinary engineering backlog. They do not collectively establish organizational sloppiness.
6. **The customer promise surface needs one versioned source of truth.** Public plan copy broadly aligns with code, but customer-specific entitlements and negotiated Enterprise commitments are unknown. Scheduled raw-event export is publicly promised but no implementing service was established in the approved monorepo. See [OI-009](controls/open-items.md#oi-009) and [OI-010](controls/open-items.md#oi-010).
7. **Scale and cost cannot be priced publicly, as expected.** The multi-store and export architecture identifies likely cost drivers and includes deliberate safeguards. Actual headroom and spend are normal first-month management baselines, not offer-stage proof requirements. See [OI-025](controls/open-items.md#oi-025) and [OI-027](controls/open-items.md#oi-027).
8. **The public record supports engineering rigor.** Intake segmentation, substantive review, broad gates, releases, public fixes, pinned dependencies, tested migration paths, and explicit failure controls are the stronger signal. Private priority and acceptance mechanics should be learned after joining. See [OI-029](controls/open-items.md#oi-029).
9. **The overall CTO decision is positive.** The audit supports proceeding enthusiastically to evaluate the people and role. It would become negative only if interviews reveal a mandate mismatch, unrealistic expectations, dishonesty about known problems, or responsibility without meaningful authority—not because private operational records were absent from a public audit.

### Decisions Now

- **Analytics-integrity abuse contract — [OI-016](controls/open-items.md#oi-016), P2, decision-needed.** Decide whether unauthenticated domain-keyed ingestion, opt-in hostname allowlisting, optional rate limits, and fail-open limiting remain accepted trade-offs. Base the decision on measured spoofing, tenant impact, false positives, and cost—not on source intent alone.
- **Role fit and mandate.** Lean yes on the technical organization. Use interviews to establish whether the founders and candidate agree on success, decision authority, resources, and ways of working. Those ordinary employment factors—not missing public operational evidence—should determine acceptance.

Start by understanding what the team is proud of, where it wants help, and whether the working relationship is right. Only direct findings or evasiveness about known problems should carry negative weight; private operational detail can wait until onboarding.
