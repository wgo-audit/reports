# Business Continuity

## Audit Question, Depth, And Evidence Boundary

Can the organization demo, deploy, operate, recover, and transfer control of self-hosted DocuSeal Community if a person, vendor, account, service, or environment disappears? This detailed review is cutoff at 2026-08-06 and uses pinned Community `3.1.7` / `a2d8b855491793870b7b4acf176d2d95ae95ff83`, registered E-001–E-018 and E-033–E-043, the [recovery/data-operations packet](../../evidence/packets/business-continuity-recovery-dataops-alerting.md), [delivery/account-transfer packet](../../evidence/packets/business-continuity-delivery-account-transfer.md), and direct source locators preserved there. Architecture and Security/Privacy artifacts were navigation and dependency evidence; their handoffs were not treated as proof.

The auditor answered BC-Q-001 on 2026-08-06: monthly availability targets are 99.5% for signing and 99% for customer onboarding; maximum data loss/RPO is two hours; all new onboarding may pause during an interruption; synchronous transactions are preferred. These are target acceptance criteria, not evidence that DocuSeal or a future deployment meets them.

Excluded: live/Cloud/Pro inspection, target account or environment access, backup/restore/failover/restart/drill, incident observation, deployment, destructive action, penetration/load testing, contract/legal/regulatory conclusions, and production approval. No secret value, private key, session material, unnecessary PII, or customer record was accessed. CodeGraph was not needed because direct source and dependency packets provided stable exact locators.

## Mandate-Relevant Strengths

- The reviewed source boundary is pinned and identifiable, and the upstream CI/image build for `3.1.7` succeeded, giving the organization a bounded starting point for controlled artifact retention; this does not identify the deployed digest or prove reproducibility.
- SQL transactions capture signer completion/events before asynchronous finalization; artifact paths use lock/retry/fail rows, and completion-ledger creation is partly idempotent. These make failure scenarios and reconciliation oracles inspectable.
- Redis can be externalized, Active Storage can use organization-selected providers, and migrations can be disabled at application boot, so the target can isolate services and introduce controlled release/recovery steps without assuming the Compose defaults.
- Webhook events/attempts and administrator resend paths provide bounded delivery evidence. `/up`, structured logs, and client error reporting are useful signals to extend into a target observability design.
- The auditor permits onboarding to pause during interruption. Continuity work therefore can prioritize safe stop, preservation, reconciliation, and controlled resume rather than inventing an unapproved manual signing path.

### Decision Insights

1. **Design recovery as one evidence set, not independent database and bucket backups.** SQL establishes workflow/provenance while blobs and keys make artifacts usable; queue state determines unfinished work. Restoring only one layer can meet infrastructure availability while violating the two-hour RPO or evidentiary integrity. Smallest next action: OI-003/OI-006 define the dependency-aware set and run a timestamped restore with cross-store/hash/key reconciliation.
2. **Use the permitted onboarding pause as a safe-stop control, not as recovery proof.** Pausing enables communication and preservation, but committed requests and queued work can remain partial. Resuming too early can duplicate or lose downstream effects. Smallest next proof: OI-003/OI-006 crash/replay/schedule/consumer drills with explicit pause, reconciliation, and controlled-resume criteria.
3. **Measure the auditor's two availability objectives at customer-visible boundaries.** `/up` can be green while database, Redis, blob, mail, webhook, signer artifact, or onboarding integration is impaired. Wrong measurement can falsely satisfy 99.5%/99%. Smallest next action: OI-014 defines service-level indicators, dependency readiness, alert delivery, incident command, and retained monthly evidence.
4. **Make successor control a production gate.** Source configurability cannot substitute for two accountable operators, break-glass custody, and vendor/account exit evidence. A single lost identity can make valid backups unusable. Smallest next proof: OI-015/OI-016 inventory every critical control and rehearse an end-to-end administrative/key/provider transfer.
5. **Retain immutable source and deployable artifacts before relying on upstream cadence.** Tags, registry images, public pages, and external components can disappear or change. Without retained verified digests, configuration schemas, and licensing/vendor routes, recovery can fail after infrastructure is restored. Smallest next action: OI-004/OI-013 retain and test the exact release supply chain and vendor escalation route.

## Bounded Conclusion And Downstream Guidance

Business Continuity supports **continue evaluation conditionally**, not production approval. Community `3.1.7` provides traceable data, queue, artifact, retry, health, logging, delivery, release, and administrator mechanisms from which a target control design can be built. It does not demonstrate either monthly availability target, the two-hour RPO, safe cross-store/key restore, queue/artifact/downstream reconciliation, alert and incident effectiveness, demo continuity, or ownership/vendor transfer. Those are production stop conditions until OI-002–OI-006/OI-013 and OI-014–OI-016 close with retained target evidence.

Contributor/Vendor Value should use the external-control inventory but must not infer ownership or staffing from Git. Maintenance Cost should price the recovery, monitoring, release, access-transfer, and exercise obligations without assuming they exist. Revenue Risk should model allowed onboarding pause and unresolved downstream reconciliation against the availability targets, without treating the source as an outage history. Expense Exposure and Scalability may reuse the dependency/test requirements but must not infer live cost or capacity.
