## Audit Question, Depth, And Evidence Boundary

This detailed review is cutoff at 2026-08-06 and uses source/history through commit `a2d8b855491793870b7b4acf176d2d95ae95ff83`, registered E-001–E-006/E-011–E-024/E-033–E-056, the completed Architecture, Code Quality, Business Continuity, Expense Exposure, and Scalability artifacts, and the [maintenance work-surface packet](../../evidence/packets/maintenance-cost-work-surfaces.md).

The 2026-08-06 continuity targets and 2026-08-07 scenario-method answer retain their later decision labels and are acceptance criteria only. Excluded: staffing interviews/inventory, time study, invoices/rates, Pro implementation, live target, build/test/deploy/upgrade/restore/scan/load exercises, alternative-product comparison, and legal/compliance determinations. Source size, change frequency, contributors, dependencies, tests, controls, and open items do not establish hours, staffing, productivity, defect rates, support levels, or money.

## Mandate-Relevant Strengths

- The pinned release, lockfiles, source topology, configured toolchains and critical workflow locators make the maintenance boundary inspectable rather than opaque.
- Five configured application jobs and the tag image job passed for the assessed snapshot, giving a useful upstream baseline while target gaps remain explicit.
- Identifiable immutable tags and commit pins provide concrete candidates for controlled intake and comparison; release frequency itself does not reduce maintenance concern.
- Source-visible configuration separates many provider/runtime choices, and existing predecessor controls already name the artifact, migration, recovery, ownership, workload and cost evidence needed before production approval.

### Decision Insights

1. **Approve the maintenance posture before customization or client binding.** Community access does not choose minimal Community, a maintained fork, vendor-supported Pro or replacement, nor intake/freeze and exit posture. The wrong choice can strand integration work or create an unowned fork. Smallest next action: serialize and answer `MC-OI-001`; leave edition/contracts/release/support/commercial proof to OI-001/OI-004/OI-005/OI-013/OI-020.
2. **Define one target-owned safe-change lane before following upstream cadence.** Frequent releases overlap mandate-critical surfaces, while upstream green CI omits target artifacts/channels/recovery/promotion. The wrong lane can be either uncontrolled uptake or unsupported stagnation. Smallest next action: implement OI-004/OI-007/OI-008 gates around an immutable candidate and representative upgrade.
3. **Use successor performance, not a skills list, as the transfer test.** The necessary domains are visible, but skill presence does not prove safe operation. Administrative/key transfer under OI-016 does not prove product change safety. Smallest next proof: serialize `MC-OI-002`, or explicitly expand OI-016, so a replacement maintainer performs the [complete evidence sequence](../../controls/maintenance/time-to-safety.md) in a non-production target.
4. **Do not price maintenance until workload and ownership are approved.** Data growth, recovery, security controls, on-call/incident, release frequency and vendor paths are work drivers, not quantities. Smallest next action: close OI-017, select the target topology/posture, then populate OI-018 with actual staffing/rates and vendor commitments.
